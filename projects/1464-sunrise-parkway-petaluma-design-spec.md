## Front yard — APPROVED design (Juan iterated to this in Grok)

The hero is a **redwood-clad planter box** that hides the existing "ugly white
brick." Everything else supports it. Order of operations: clear the red-tag
electrical/plumbing first (conceal the exposed white pipe/hose during that
work), then the yard.

1. **Redwood planter box (the design element).** Clad the white-brick planter
   in horizontal **construction-heart redwood** (1x6 or 2x6), add a top cap
   board, attach clean. Redwood is local, naturally rot-resistant, warms up
   the beige house, photographs great. Cosmetic — no permit.
2. **Mulch.** Dark brown bark / hardwood mulch, 2–3" deep, across the beds.
   Refresh sharp, spade-cut edges for the "done / professional" look.
3. **River-rock edging.** Rounded gray/tan 3–6" river rock in a single neat
   row framing the mulched areas **and** running both sides of the central
   concrete walkway — turns a plain slab into an intentional bordered path.
4. **Small grass patch** in the central pointed area beside the walkway (the
   exact spot Juan pointed at) — the soft, friendly "design-friendly" green
   accent. Small = low water/mowing, but not drought-tolerant, so keep it to
   that one patch.
5. **Layered drought-tolerant planting** (Petaluma = Zone 9b, Mediterranean):
   - **Back / against house:** upright rosemary + Ceanothus (California lilac)
     for evergreen structure; Pathfinder juniper only in a back corner where
     the tall columnar shape fits (it grows 15–20 ft — keep it out of the
     small middle bed).
   - **Middle:** lavender (English/Spanish) — purple blooms + fragrance, the
     "wow" color.
   - **Front / edges:** Dymondia margaretae "Silver Carpet" groundcover along
     the path strips, plus low succulents (echeveria, sedum, aeonium) and
     creeping thyme for texture.
