# Direct Mail Viability — The Questions To Ask Before We Kill It

> Created 2026-08-01, in response to: *"Direct mail is our workhorse but now we
> think it's dead — what are the questions that should be asked?"*
>
> Source data: `marketing-postcard-kpis.md` (Redstone/postcard workbook, 24 tabs),
> `deal-pattern-buybox.md` (56 historical deals), `twin-home-buyer-company.md`.

## The Frame

**The question is not "is direct mail dead." It is "did we ever actually run
direct mail?"**

What we ran: a statewide spray across 13 area groups and ~10 list types, with
only ~15% aimed at the proven East Bay sub-$1M buy-box and ~45% aimed off-box
(including the exact premium Peninsula markets where *every historical loss*
came from) — combined with a conversion step that turned 30 interested sellers
into 3 offers.

That is not a channel test. Killing "direct mail" off this data is killing the
wrong thing, and it discards the ~$12.9K of data/automation infrastructure and
216 days of list-building that a real test would sit on top of.

Equally: nobody has verified that mail was ever the workhorse. That claim is
unsourced (see Q2).

---

## TIER 1 — Is the verdict even real? (data integrity)

1. **What exactly are we calling "direct mail"?**
   77,763 pieces across 13 area groups × ~10 list types is not one channel —
   it's ~30 channels averaged into a single number. **What is the response rate
   and cost-per-contract for the buy-box slice ONLY** (10,994 on-box addresses;
   specifically the 2,786-piece "Stack — Oakland/Emeryville 7 core zips" drop,
   which produced the most interested callers per piece of anything we sent)?

2. **Was mail ever actually the workhorse?**
   Which channel(s) produced the 56 historical flips / ~$12.8M gross profit?
   This is still an open question in `twin-home-buyer-company.md`. If mail never
   sourced those deals, "our workhorse died" is factually wrong and we're
   grieving a channel that was never load-bearing.

3. **Does our attribution capture mail at all?**
   Postcard lands in February, seller calls in June — possibly after a Google
   search — does that log as mail, PPC, or organic? Note that ~41% of responders
   asked to be removed (19 DNM + 19 DNC), which proves the mail is being *read*.
   Delayed/indirect response is the norm for mail and the hardest thing to track.

4. **Do we trust the sheet?**
   The workbook reports conflicting date windows (Dec 3 2025–May 29 2026 vs.
   Nov 6 2025–Mar 28 2026) and an "avg 34 days mail→contract" alongside 0
   contracts recorded. Reconcile before deciding. We should not kill a $50K
   channel off a spreadsheet that contradicts itself.

---

## TIER 2 — Is it the channel, or is it us? (execution)

5. **Targeting — the biggest single variable.**
   ~15% on-box, ~45% off-box (SF, Peninsula, South Bay, Central Valley, Sonoma,
   Solano, Marin, LA ~2,764 pieces, Lake Elsinore). **What is the response rate
   and interested-rate on-box vs. off-box?** If on-box responds at 4x off-box,
   the list is dead, not the channel.

6. **The conversion cliff — the real leak.**
   30 interested → **3 offers made** → 0 acquired. **Why did 27 interested
   sellers never receive an offer?** A ~10% offer-sent rate kills any channel,
   at any cost per lead. This is the finding that matters most and it has
   nothing to do with mail.

7. **Offer price and authority.**
   Seller notes: "our offer was too low," "reaction is sarcastic, ended call
   abruptly," lost one to an investor at $350K. Who owns the offer number? What
   is the written max-offer rule? Are we underwriting to Juan's 85% ceiling or
   materially below it?

8. **Speed to lead.**
   Stated company rule is 5 minutes. **What is the actual median time-to-first-
   contact on mail leads?** The notes are full of "Callback made. No answer."

9. **Follow-up cadence / SLA.**
   How many touches over how many days before an "interested" lead is
   abandoned? Direct-mail deals commonly close on touch 5–12. Right now leads
   visibly slip to "no answer" and die.

10. **Rep-level accountability.**
    Calls by rep: Jose 142, Cherry 76, Juan (Live) 33, Thea 28, Karen 12,
    John AI 8, Arly 1. **What is interested→offer→contract conversion BY REP?**
    Also flag: the CEO personally took 33 calls — that's a role-design problem,
    not a marketing problem.

11. **Contract durability.**
    Cancelled-contract rate reads 50% (the one recorded contract — Lake
    Elsinore, off-box — canceled). Even when we get one, we can't hold it. Why?

---

## TIER 3 — Did the market actually change? (is mail structurally worse?)

12. **Touch count per address.**
    Did we mail 77,763 addresses once, or ~20,000 addresses four times?
    Response climbs materially through touches 3–5. If we ran one-and-done
    across a huge universe, we never gave the channel its mechanism.

13. **List saturation / competitive volume.**
    How many other investors are mailing the same NOD / lien / tax-delinquent /
    absentee lists in our 7 core zips, and has that increased? Distress lists are
    the most-mailed lists in the industry.

14. **Universe size — the honest ceiling.**
    How many qualifying distressed/absentee owners actually exist inside the
    buy-box? If the on-box universe is only ~11K addresses, **mail cannot be the
    workhorse by arithmetic** — it's a supplement to MLS/PPC/network, and
    expecting a primary channel from it was the original error.

15. **Cost per piece.**
    $0.544 Redstone / $0.65 all-in. What's current market rate? What does a
    second mail house quote on the identical list? Is a cheaper letter/yellow-
    letter format better per response than a postcard for distressed sellers?

16. **Postage and format changes.**
    Any USPS rate changes or format/deliverability shifts since Dec 2025 that
    moved the economics? (3.4% of uploads dropped as bad/dupe — what's the
    current deliverability and return-mail rate?)

---

## TIER 4 — The CEO question: capital allocation

17. **What's the cost per signed contract on every OTHER channel?**
    MLS acquisition pipeline, PPC, cold call/dialer, agent & wholesaler network,
    SEO. Mail's competition isn't "zero" — it's the next-best dollar. Without
    this table, killing mail is a vibe, not an allocation decision.

18. **What's break-even?**
    At ~$228K average gross per deal (net still unverified), what is the maximum
    we're *allowed* to spend to acquire one deal? Until that number is written
    down, "too expensive" is undefined.

19. **If we shut it off, what fills the top of funnel in 90 days — and who owns it?**
    No channel gets killed without a named replacement and a named owner.

20. **What's the shutdown / restart cost?**
    ~$12.9K (25%) of the $50.5K was tooling and data infra — BigQuery,
    DealMachine, Make.com, Property Radar, Profit Dial, Auto Genius. Some of it
    dies with mail, some doesn't, some serves other channels. Which subscriptions
    actually get cancelled, and what does it cost to rebuild the list pipeline if
    we restart in six months?

21. **Cash-phase test:** we are in a 90-day cash-stabilization reset. Does *any*
    version of this pass the "protect cash first" non-negotiable, and does the
    spend clear the >$1–2K review rule?

## The One Question That Settles It

> **"If we mail only the 7 core Oakland/Emeryville zips (94608, 94607, 94612,
> 94606, 94601, 94621, 94603), 3 touches, with a 5-minute speed-to-lead SLA and
> a written max-offer rule — what is our cost per signed contract over 90 days?"**

We have never run that test. Not once. Everything we know about "direct mail" at
Twin Home Buyer comes from a statewide spray with a broken conversion step.

---

## Recommendation

**Don't kill it. Shrink it to the test.**

1. **Cut off-box mail to zero this week.** ~45% of mail volume is aimed at
   geographies where we have never made money and, on the Peninsula, where every
   historical loss lives. This is the highest-confidence cost cut available and
   it requires no new information.
2. **Fix the conversion step in parallel — it's free.** Written max-offer rule +
   owner, 5-minute speed-to-lead enforced, and a defined touch cadence for
   "interested." These fixes pay off across *every* channel, so they're worth
   doing even if mail dies.
3. **Run one concentrated 90-day on-box test** on the 7 core zips at a hard
   budget cap (~$5–6K total, ~3 touches on ~2,800 addresses), with kill criteria
   written before the first drop.
4. **Write the kill criteria in advance.** Proposed: if 90 days of on-box,
   3-touch, SLA-enforced mail produces < 1 signed contract, direct mail is dead
   for Twin Home Buyer and we say so permanently and reallocate.

2. Name the owner of the max-offer number and the follow-up SLA. This is the
