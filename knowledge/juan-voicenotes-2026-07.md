## 2. How Juan talks to sellers (signature moves)

**Rapport first.** He brings goodies or a beer, compliments the house ("Man,
you are spotless", "It's a great house"), asks where they're moving and how
long they've lived there, and tells his own stories (moving every 2 years with
his wife to use the $250K/$500K exclusion, digging a 12-ft basement under his
own house).

**Makes the seller name the number first.**
- "The magical thing is price, right? Where do you need to be on price?"
- "Something between where you want to be and where we want to be."
- "What number do you think would make you happy?" (Seller: "2 million."
  Juan: "Yeah, so would I. I'd be jumping up and down.")
- "Why don't you roll with us?" / "So you're not ready to roll with us today?"

**Radical transparency is his pitch.**
- "I don't want to bullshit you."
- "Just full transparency again…"
- "I can sing a song and tell you exactly what you want to hear." (He
  won't.)
- "For me, transparency is everything… if it works for them, great, and if it
  doesn't, it doesn't."
- "We're not for everyone. A cash buyer is not for everyone."
- "We come very friendly. If it works, great, and if it doesn't, then it
  doesn't. But we'd like the opportunity… we'd even like to hear a
  counter-offer."
- "There's no pressure here. I'm not a used car sales guy." / "Take your
  time… make the right decision for you and your family."

**Credibility lines he uses.** "26 years", "thousands of homes, mostly Bay
Area, a lot in Sacramento", "over 100 reviews online", "I'm a licensed
contractor, General B. I'm not a realtor or broker", "I own the business."
Buying entity on the grant deed: **Equity Track**.

**Analogies.** Keeping a Berkeley tenant in place is "like walking into a
casino and dumping all your money on the table." Overdoing an estate sale:
"you're putting way more effort than the stuff is worth."

## 3. The cash-offer contract, as Juan explains it

1. Same as any sale: a **neutral title company** holds the money and works for both sides.
2. **$500 deposit** to escrow, which triggers the **preliminary title report (2–3
   days)**: ownership, liens, encumbrances, taxes owed.
3. Once title says "clear to close", **the seller picks the closing date**. "Within
   literally seven days we can get the deal done", or two years if they want.
4. **No financing contingency** (cash). **Non-contingent, as-is**: "I'm not asking
   you to clear the rats or cut trees or change windows." His own walkthrough
   *is* the inspection.
5. **No commission, no closing costs.** "The money that we give you, that's the
   money you get." We pay transfer tax, title/escrow, notary and misc.
6. Flexible **stay-behind / post-close occupancy**. Seller can **leave
   belongings**. We offer them to Goodwill / Salvation Army / Boys & Girls
   Club first ("now with AI we'll send this email to 20 of them") and the
   seller keeps the donation tax receipt.
7. We'll even **buy the seller's car** (kept separate from the house price).
8. Docs go out electronically (SignNow); **Cherry** sends them and follows up
   on the offer.
9. He reminds sellers of the **§121 primary-residence exclusion** ($250K
   single / $500K married).

## 4. Deal doctrine heard in these notes

- **Tenants:** won't buy with a tenant staying ("the house cannot be sold with
  her in it"; a tenant "significantly drops" value in Berkeley).
  - One contingency only: an attorney-written move-out agreement with the
    tenant, with the **rent board notified** ("our attorney would not allow
    us to move forward without it").
  - Budget **$20–50K cash-for-keys**.
  - Notify the tenant only once the buyer is locked in, then hold a respectful
    sit-down meeting.
  - War stories: an Ellis Act case where the judge sent them to settle ($80K
    to move one tenant), and Berkeley tenants who withheld rent for 3 years
    over a "mold" claim.
- **Protect the seller from bait-and-switch:** if another buyer promises the
  tenant can stay, tell the seller to demand a **$50K earnest money deposit**
  (~3%). A $500 deposit means the buyer can walk away for free.
- **No joint ventures.** "I would not do a joint venture. I've had bad
  experiences with that in the past." He buys outright or passes. On a
  development lot, he'd rather **buy and resell to a developer**.
- **Development math:** it's a 3–4-year project and needs **$3–4M of upside**,
  because "there's a chance you take half, a chance you break even."
  - When COVID hit, he had 3 developments going and paid **$100–150K** just to
    get out of them.
  - He has 2 developments running now (Jul 2026).
  - His biggest macro worry: **AI replacing the tech jobs** of Bay Area buyers.
- **Below-grade square footage is expensive:** egress wells, drainage,
  ceiling height, holding up the house.
- **Always see the property:** "An investor that passed away told me: 'Juan,
  if you can't go see a property within two hours, don't ever buy
  anything.' I've stuck to that and it works."
- **Field-note habit:** he records the address first, then exterior condition,
  red flags (an "addition that looks like it's not legal") and foundation.
