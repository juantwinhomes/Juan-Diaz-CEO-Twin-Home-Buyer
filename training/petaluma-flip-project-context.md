# Project Context: Petaluma Flip (Red-Tagged House)

Training reference for Juan's CEO Twin. This captures the decisions already made in
conversation so the Twin does not re-litigate them or drift in future sessions.

## Permits & Licensing (settled facts — do not contradict)

- The house is **red-tagged**. Electrical and plumbing corrections are the first
  legal priority before anything else matters.
- Juan pulls the permits himself under his **General B license**. A B contractor
  can pull permits covering two or more trades on the same job — do NOT tell him
  he needs a separate C-10 electrician or C-36 plumber to pull permits.
- Scope is **like-for-like**: no fixtures moved, no walls moved, no rerouted lines.
  Kitchen remodel + bathroom remodel + light electrical + plumbing.
- Petaluma Building Department: 707-778-4301, open Mon–Thu, everything goes
  through the online Permits & Planning Hub. No general expedited track.
- **Landscaping requires no permit** in Petaluma.

## Front Yard Design (final direction — locked)

The yard design went through many iterations. This is where it landed:

1. **Redwood-clad planter box = the hero feature.** Horizontal redwood boards
   (1x8 or 2x6 construction heart) with a top cap, cladding the existing white
   brick raised bed to hide it completely.
2. **Grass goes in the small planter-box area next to the concrete path**
   (the spot Juan pointed to) — NOT spread across the big open areas.
3. **Dark brown bark mulch everywhere else**, 2–3" deep, sharp spade-cut edges.
4. **Rocks: minimal only.** A single neat row bordering the grass patch is
   acceptable. Juan explicitly rejected river-rock edging around the mulch and
   path ("that would look horrible") — never propose it again.
5. **Planting inside the box**: layered — tall upright evergreen structure in
   back, lavender + white/pink bloom color in the middle, succulents/groundcover
   texture in front. All drought-tolerant, Zone 9b appropriate.
