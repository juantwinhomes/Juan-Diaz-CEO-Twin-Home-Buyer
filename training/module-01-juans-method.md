## 1. Why you’re training

- Making outbound calls right away is not the goal.
- The goal is to learn a skill you can repeat: handling after-hours calls using AI.
- Getting through technical and conversation roadblocks with AI is a core skill for handling calls.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “the goal was not about making outbound calls immediately, but rather learning a replicable skill to handle after-hours calls using AI”
> “navigating through technical and conversational roadblocks using AI represents a core competency for future call management”

## 2. Master one skill at a time (the basketball method)

- Learn like a beginner basketball player. Master one skill, then move to the next. Don’t advance before you’re ready.
- Think of Steph Curry shooting three-pointers: repetition, correction, and teaching yourself.
- Don’t rush into live calls.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “Juan outlined a step-by-step training method where reps master single skills sequentially.”
> “a basketball-inspired training model comparing skill acquisition to Steph Curry shooting three-pointers, emphasizing repetition, correction, and self-education over rushing into live calls”
> “must master skills sequentially like a beginner basketball player rather than advancing prematurely”

## 3. Practice with AI first

- Use AI to practice hard seller objections before you face them on a real call.
- Make your practice scenarios realistic.
- Use AI to troubleshoot technical problems too.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “Juan demonstrated using AI to troubleshoot technical issues and practice complex seller objections.”
> “emphasizing the creation of realistic practice scenarios”

## 4. Research every inbound lead with AI before you respond

1. Get the caller’s name and the property address.
2. Put the details into your web browser.
3. Ask more than one AI tool: ChatGPT, Grok and Claude.
4. Then respond to the seller.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “the inbound lead process requiring property research using AI before making calls”
> “requiring staff to first obtain the caller's name and address, input the details into a web browser, and query multiple AI tools including ChatGPT, Grock [Grok], and Claude”
> “Staff must gather caller details and query multiple AI tools before responding.”

## 5. Never claim to know property details you found online

- Say where the information came from. Example: “According to Redfin…”
- Never talk as if you know the property yourself.
- Naming the source keeps you from sounding robotic.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “agents must attribute online property details to external sources rather than claiming direct knowledge”
> “frame online information using phrases like "according to Redfin" to avoid sounding robotic”

## 6. Use AI as a guide, not a script

- Treat AI as a guide for the conversation.
- Don’t read rigid scripts.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “Juan emphasized treating AI as a conversational guide rather than reading rigid scripts.”

## 7. No prices over the phone

- Never give a property price over the phone.
- It doesn’t work. Every property needs a physical inspection first.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “the strict policy against providing property prices over the phone”
> “giving prices over the phone is ineffective because properties require physical inspection”

## 8. Cash buyer or agent? Tell the truth.

- A cash buyer is a fit when the house needs major repairs.
- If the seller has no financial pressure, recommend listing with an agent.

> From the Oct 19 notes:
> “Juan Diaz responded that cash buyers suit major repair needs”
> “cash buyers should recommend listing with an agent if clients face no financial constraints”
