From postcard to closing

The postcard funnel is steep but real: 117 leads, 9 reached an appointment or evaluation, and 1 closed — Denise Marks, a March "Postcard" lead tagged Acquired & Deal Closed. None of the current relaunch batches has produced a confirmed close yet — those leads are still young.

One close on 117 leads is a 0.9% lead→acquisition rate on tags alone. A single postcard deal can clear the whole $22.9K mail cost, so the channel can pay — but the relaunch batches need another 30–60 days to show their closings before judging ROI.

Which postcard worked

REI Blackbook

Postcards attribute better than the rest of direct mail — 39 of 117 leads (33%) carry a campaign name, versus 14% across all direct mail. Where it's tagged, "Postcard Liens" dominates with 20 of 39, confirming liens as the workhorse list.

Campaign on postcard leads — 39 of 117 tagged
Still the gap
78 of 117 postcard leads (67%) have no campaign tag. Better than the channel average, but until every postcard lead is auto-stamped with its batch, "Postcard Liens vs Ugly Houses vs Tax Delinquent" can't be ranked by close rate.
The bottom line — postcards

Efficient to mail, liens-led, too young to judge on ROI.

Postcards mail cheaply and consistently: 47,510 pieces at $0.48, scaling hard in June. They return leads at roughly $359 each and attribute better than any other mail format — a third carry a campaign, and liens drive the response.

But conversion is where patience is required: 1 confirmed close on 117 leads, and that close predates the current relaunch. The May–Jul batches have produced 64 leads and no confirmed deal yet — normal for a channel with a 30–90 day close cycle.

Watch, don't cut. Keep mailing the liens postcard lists, stamp each lead with its batch, and re-pull closings in 60 days. That turns "one close so far" into a real per-list ROI you can scale on.
