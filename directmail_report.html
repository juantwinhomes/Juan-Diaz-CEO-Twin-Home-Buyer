01

The spend

QuickBooks

Direct mail is the largest acquisition channel in the books — $124,330 year to date, 39% of all acquisition marketing spend. It front-loaded hard in Q1, went nearly dark in April, and rebuilt through June under the Redstone relaunch.

AM- Direct Mail spend, by month — $124,330 total
Reading it
The April cliff ($759) is the program pause. Everything from May on is the new Redstone-tracked era, which is why piece counts only exist from then forward.
02

The mail itself

Monday.com · Redstone

On the Iriga Operating System, every relaunch batch is logged with quantity and cost. Twelve batches since May put 47,803 pieces in the mail at $0.49 apiece — cost per piece falling from $0.61 to $0.40 as batches scaled.

List DNA
Nearly every batch is built from lien data pulled in PropertyRadar — utility, HOA, and county/city liens, filtered to 45–50%+ equity, non-corporate owners, pre-2017 transfers. This is, functionally, a liens program.

04

From mailbox to closing

REI Blackbook tags

This is where the honesty check lives. Of 377 direct-mail leads, the tags trace a steep funnel down to one confirmed acquisition (Denise Marks, a postcard lead) plus two under contract. At $124K spent, that is the number to pressure-test — not celebrate.

Direct-mail conversion funnel
Caveat that matters
These stages are read from hand-applied tags, which are inconsistent — some leads carry "Sold" next to "Declined Offer." Cost per confirmed acquisition looks like $124K, but with attribution this incomplete, treat it as a ceiling to disprove, not a verdict.
05

Campaign attribution

REI Blackbook

Only 54 of 377 leads (14%) carry a campaign name at all. Where it exists, liens dominate — "Postcard Liens" and "EQT Liens – Check" alone are nearly half. The other 86% arrive untagged, which is the single biggest gap in measuring this channel.

Campaign names on direct-mail leads — 54 tagged
The gap
323 of 377 direct-mail leads have no campaign tag. Until each lead is stamped with the batch it answered, true cost-per-deal by list type can't be computed — it has to be reconstructed by hand, as this report did.
The bottom line

A liens machine that mails well and measures poorly.

The production side is healthy: 47,803 pieces at $0.49 each, cost per piece falling as volume scales, built on disciplined lien lists. The channel returns leads on schedule — 377 this year, ~$330 each.

The measurement side is where the money leaks. Only 1 acquisition is provable from tags, and 86% of leads have no campaign attached. The channel may well be profitable — a single wholesale or flip clears the $124K — but right now nobody can prove which list or batch earned it.

The fix is one wire: stamp every direct-mail lead with its Redstone batch as it lands, and reconcile closings against QuickBooks. Do that, and this becomes a channel you can optimize by list type instead of one you audit by hand.
